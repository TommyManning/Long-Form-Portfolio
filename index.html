Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

I have been challenged and pushed in this class to more and more create a message and rhetorical appeal/idea without words which is a new concept to me. Originally it was difficult, especially for the logo project to get a point across with few to no words, and a minimal design but it has encouraged me to be more conscious of how the smaller details affect the overall message of a creation.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

Utilizing principles like contrast, font, alignment, repetition, etc. was not a practice I thought I would become more conscious and aware of but this class has taught me the importance of minor details that have major impacts on a design. With the logo design for example, I was able to fine tune the logos simply by changing the font of one of the sections to contrast to the other used font, so that I utilized both principles at once. For the good design project, I utilized the contrasting red colors of the poster itself with the text boxes to create further differentiation through something as simple as hues.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

Through meaningful dialogue and listening, I was able to see what skills and perspectives my peers were taking in regards to different projects. Jumping into the design right away with a narrow mind on what the project should be. I appreciated that their approach to the projects were different then mine. My classmates have helped me in the workshop to open up my creativity and thought process so that I can be more willing to extend beyond what I thought I could do with design. With the Design Fuego, I learned from Nate how to change the font color and the background color, and drew on other ideas from my group such as establishing a more refined and definitive color palette for the logo project and simplifying the designs.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

Having used the program Canva and not using InDesign, I was able to take the tools and experiences from both and use them to direct how I went about redesigning my logos. This class has inundated me with the notion that my first creation is not going to be perfect and nor should it. This mindset, whereby there is always room for improvement, rather than starting off with a failure has opened me up to be excited to take criticism and inspiration from others and revise. Specifically with the UX design project, my first interview stung a little, but then with the second and third I realized that my friends were simply trying to help me improve and were giving their genuine reactions. Their honesty helped me to be honest about how it was not originally my best work and that, as long as I fix it, that is okay.

5. Learn to present your work.

Looking back on presentations, I have always presented information that I have known and expanded upon during the presentation, but never presented on work in a way that I had to “prove my work.” The presentations gave me the opportunity to own up to the work I have done, meaning I had to each project with care and intention so that I could answer for the design choices I have met.

Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

With more writing in these short form projects I was further challenged to combine my novel design skills with my writing skills. Projects like the infographic and advertisement were great opportunities to use informative and concise language to pair with the design to make it impactful and effective.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

Utilizing principles like font and alignment were specifically present in the advertisement, infographic, and accessibility project. These projects had challenged me to use shapes outside of the normal textbox and fit and align the text to them, such as the triangle in th accessibility project. With more written text in the short form projects, I had to be ever more conscious of how contrasting sizes and fonts would be beneficial to the reader and draw them in. For these aforementioned projects, I worked to balance and bring order through different fonts and the alignment of shapes and other design elements.

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

Having the opportunity to sit with the same peers week in and week out for this long has been extremely beneficial. In the beginning I was able to take constructive criticism well from them. With time, this experience was further elevated, as each person was better able to critique my pieces and give suggestions about how they think it falls short of what they know I can do. For example, with the Advertisement project, I received helpful and critical advice on how to better get my message across and how to make the image pop out more so that the viewer is immediately drawn in and hooked on the ad. For example, although it is obviously a soldier, the ad does not make it apparent that it is for the Army, so at the suggestion of my classmate, I updated it to have the Army logo in a way that was subtle but apparent.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

Similarly to the previous question, with more experience in engaging in workshop where there was constructive criticism, I become ever more accepting and grateful for revisions that needed to be changed. Projects like the advertisement and infographic gave me further belief in the need for revision, regardless of how good I thought the design from the start was. My mindset continues to be shaped by my classmates and thus my revisions are more intentional and my ability to change a large portion of the project has also been solidified For example, they enojoyed the visuals of the infographic project, but pushed me to change it in a way that gave more data/info not just explanations of the problems, so included the data box and some bolded sections about the problem. .

5. Learn to present your work.

Listening in on the focus groups and having more and more opportunities to talk with those sitting around me has made me more comfortable in presenting and defending my work, while being open and gracious in accepting feedback and help. For example, one of the students projects had a title and subtitle that did not blend well, and the focus group told them that. Based on that discussion, I approached my infographic title to combine different elemnts for the main and subtitles in a way that did ont take away from either.

Course Goals


1. Consider the role of rhetoric, technology, and ethics in acts of making.

Throughout this project, I became increasingly aware of how rhetorical choices intersect with both technological tools and ethical considerations. I learned that every design choice—whether a color, symbol, or layout—carries persuasive weight, even when I am not consciously attempting to persuade. Using digital tools pushed me to think critically about how different features influence the messages I construct.

2. Leverage Design Principles.

By taking time to intentionally apply the princiles I have learned over the semester principles, I gained more control over the effectiveness and readability of the final product

3. Engage with others in a workshop environment.

Participating in the workshop environment for this semester taught me the value of genuine collaboration. My classmates noticed things I did not—such as areas where my message felt unclear or where the layout felt visually unbalanced. Instead of seeing critique as a setback, I learned to view it as shared problem-solving. Hearing others talk through their ideas also pushed me to think beyond the first design option that came to mind and consider alternate approaches. The workshop became a space not only to refine my work, but to exchange creative strategies, learn new techniques, and build confidence in discussing design decisions openly.



4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

Revision for this assignment involved more than just visual tweaks; it required rethinking how the design functioned across formats and platforms. Shifting from rough sketches to digital mock-ups helped me see what worked in theory versus what worked in practice.This iterative process reinforced the understanding that revision is not a single step but a sustained mindset that strengthens the final product every time.

5. Learn to present your work.

Presenting these projects asked me to not just show what I made, but to articulate why I made those choices. Explaining my rationale aloud helped me better understand my own creative approach and made future projects easier to plan. It also taught me to communicate with clarity and confidence, knowing that the ability to present my work is just as important as the work itself.
